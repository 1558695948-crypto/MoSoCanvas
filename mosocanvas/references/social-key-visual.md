# Social Key Visual

Load this file after confirming the task is a social campaign key visual or poster.

## Evaluate at the carrier

Define the real viewing condition before judging detail:

- thumbnail or feed: test primary recognition at small scale;
- cover or poster: test hierarchy at intended distance;
- print: test ink count, minimum line weight, material, bleed, and authoritative text assets;
- multi-size campaign: preserve proposition and visual system, but permit recomposition when aspect ratios change.

Do not confuse a beautiful full-size image with a functioning thumbnail.

## Build the first-read hierarchy

Separate:

1. **First read:** one identity, subject, or proposition that survives the smallest required scale.
2. **Second read:** context, action, or supporting copy.
3. **Discovery layer:** texture, easter egg, secondary logo, or cultural detail.

Unknown brands usually need stronger name recognition than mature brands. A small environmental wordmark may be elegant but fail acquisition. Solve this with a second editorial title layer, not necessarily a large opaque label.

## Translate references into mechanisms

For each reference, record:

- `extract`: palette roles, contrast structure, grain, line behavior, spatial rhythm, lighting logic, typography hierarchy, or narrative distance;
- `do_not_copy`: exact subject, identity, composition, protected mark, or artist signature;
- `function`: what the mechanism does in this task;
- `failure_risk`: how it becomes a generic imitation.

Combine references by shared mechanisms, not by averaging their objects. “Blue + pink” is not a sufficient analysis if the references depend on ink density, large dark silhouettes, restrained practical light, or quiet narrative tension.

## Avoid template diagnosis

Do not maintain a blacklist of styles. Diagnose observable replacement of proposition by convention:

- every edge receives neon despite no lighting reason;
- black-gold materials encode generic luxury instead of the brand promise;
- unrelated UI charts, color chips, or studio props signal “design” without serving the message;
- regional motifs are stacked as tourist icons instead of forming one spatial or semantic mechanism;
- roughness is applied at equal intensity so intentional disorder becomes undifferentiated noise.

Always name a counterexample where the same convention could be valid.

## Typography and logos

- Treat exact text and official logos as identity-sensitive assets.
- Prefer deterministic typography or compositing after image generation.
- If text is generated inside an image, mark spelling and glyph fidelity as unverified until inspected.
- Use type contrast to create hierarchy: size, width, weight, spacing, alignment, and language pairing before decoration.
- Keep public-service copy from becoming a warning poster by controlling scale, spacing, tone, and brand relationship—not by hiding it.

## Cultural material

- Ask which locality, audience, source, or review authority applies.
- Prefer structural mechanisms over motif piles: a Huizhou courtyard can become negative space; a seal can connect place, brand, and meaning.
- Do not invent historical claims, seal forms, minority symbols, or ritual meanings.
- Use authoritative logo and text files for production; an image mockup is not a print master.

## Observable pass conditions

Write conditions such as:

- Brand name remains readable at a 160 px-wide preview.
- The first read is the product promise, not decorative props.
- All required copy is exact and within the print-safe area.
- Reference texture is present without copying its subject or composition.
- The red light affects the right-facing surfaces consistently.
- The regional element is recognizable through architecture, not through an added label.

Do not write “looks高级”, “more artistic”, or “young users will like it” as pass conditions.
